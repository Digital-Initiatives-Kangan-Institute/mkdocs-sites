14. Selecting Hardware for a Small Project

14.1 Scenario

A friend wants to build a device that will sit in their lounge room and run all day, every day, without using much electricity or making any noise. It needs to:

  • Run a lightweight operating system suitable for low-power hardware
  • Connect to the home Wi-Fi network
  • Stay on 24 hours a day, 7 days a week
  • Be as small and inexpensive as possible
  • Fit inside a small case they plan to 3D print

They do not need a screen, keyboard, or mouse once it is set up — they will connect to it remotely from their laptop. Their budget for the computer and everything needed to get it running is $75 AUD.

14.2 Exercise 1: Identify the Right Hardware

Instructions

Your friend is describing requirements that do not suit a traditional desktop or laptop computer. Research what type of small, low-cost, low-power computer would meet their needs. Your answer should include:

  • The name of the computer you recommend and why it fits the requirements
  • Where your friend can buy one
  • What else they need to purchase to get it running (power supply, storage, adapters)
  • The total estimated cost

Explain your choice in a way your friend (who is not technical) would understand.

Hint - Think small

This is not a job for a regular desktop PC or laptop. Look into single-board computers. Consider size, power consumption, and cost. Look at what is available from Australian electronics retailers like Core Electronics, Little Bird, or Jaycar.

14.3 Exercise 2: Choose and Install an Operating System

Instructions

The computer you selected in Exercise 1 does not come with an operating system installed. Research and answer the following:

  • Which operating system would you install on this device and why?
  • What tools or software do you need to prepare the operating system?
  • Write a numbered list of the steps your friend would follow to get the operating system onto the device.
  • Your friend does not have a spare monitor, keyboard, or mouse. How will they set it up and connect to it?
Hint - Preparing the OS

The operating system for this type of computer is usually written to a microSD card using a tool on your main computer. Think about what needs to be configured before the first boot so your friend can connect to it over the network without ever plugging in a monitor or keyboard. Look up the term "headless setup".

14.4 Exercise 3: Research Project Possibilities

Instructions

Your friend is still deciding what the device will actually do. Research three different projects that could run on this hardware 24/7. For each project, write:

  • What the project does
  • A real-world benefit of running it
  • Whether any extra hardware or accessories are needed

Choose projects that are appropriate for a device of this size and power. Running a Minecraft server, for example, would not be suitable — explain why in your own words.

Hint - What runs well on low-power hardware

Look for projects that process network traffic, serve simple web pages, log data from sensors, stream media, or run automated tasks. These types of services are lightweight and do not need much CPU or RAM. Avoid anything involving video processing, 3D rendering, or hosting large databases. Search for popular home server or do-it-yourself tech projects.
