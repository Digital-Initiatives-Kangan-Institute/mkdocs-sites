# Introduction to IoT & Microcontroller Fundamentals
**The Internet of Things (IoT)** is the practice of embedding sensing, computation, and network connectivity into everyday physical objects: a soil-moisture sensor that waters a garden automatically, a shipping pallet that reports its own temperature, a wearable that tracks a heartbeat and syncs it to a phone. What makes a "thing" capable of any of this is almost always a **microcontroller**: a small, self-contained computer on a single chip, cheap and power-efficient enough to be embedded permanently inside a physical object rather than housed on a desk.

Every IoT device, however different it looks on the outside, is built from the same handful of underlying layers: **electricity** (the physical voltages and currents everything else rides on), **components** (resistors, capacitors, transistors, sensors, the physical building blocks), a **microcontroller** (the chip that reads those components and decides what to do), **code** (the language and logic running on that chip), and **communication** (getting a result off the device and somewhere useful). This guide examines each layer in depth: first in platform-agnostic terms, then through a concrete worked example, and finally compared across several microcontroller families: the **ESP32** (this guide's primary reference platform), **AVR/Arduino Uno**, **STM32**, **RP2040 (Raspberry Pi Pico)**, and **PIC**. The same underlying principles (Ohm's Law, an ADC's resolution, a Wi-Fi connection's lifecycle) apply on every board, even as the exact syntax or pin numbers change.
