# Task 10 - Planning a Website

## Identify the Requirements

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using a document or notepad**, read the client brief below and write a list of **every** requirement the client has asked for, one requirement per line.

    !!! quote "Client brief: Paws and Claws Pet Grooming"
        Hi, I run a small pet grooming salon called Paws and Claws and I'd like our first website. We need four pages: a home page, a services page, an about us page and a contact page.

        The home page should welcome visitors, tell them where we are and when we're open. Please put the opening hours in a table so they're easy to read, and add a link to the services page.

        The services page should list each of our grooming services with a photo, a short description and the price.

        On the about us page I'd like to tell the story of how the salon started and the year we opened, and introduce our groomers with a photo and a few words about each of them.

        The contact page needs a form where people can enter their name, email address and a message. It doesn't need to actually send yet, my nephew is going to set that part up later.

        Every page should have a menu so people can get to the other pages, and our logo at the top should take people back to the home page.

??? question "Hint"
    Read the brief sentence by sentence, and look out for sentences that contain more than one requirement. The words **"in a table"**, **"with a photo"** and **"every page"** each describe *how* something must be done, and are requirements too. You should end up with well over ten requirements.

## Sitemap on Paper

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using pen and paper**, draw a visual sitemap for the Paws and Claws website.

    - draw one box for each page
    - write the page name and its file name in each box
    - connect the boxes with lines to show the hierarchy

??? question "Hint"
    The home page always sits at the top of the sitemap. Every page the client listed in the brief sits one level below it. Remember that the home page's file name is always `index.html`.

## Sitemap with a Diagramming Tool

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using draw.io** (`https://app.diagrams.net/`), recreate your Paws and Claws sitemap digitally.

    - add a box for each page, with its page name and file name
    - under each page name, add a short note of the main content on that page, taken from your requirements list
    - export the finished sitemap as a `PNG` image

??? question "Hint"
    The steps to create and export a diagram are in the [Creating a Visual Sitemap with draw.io](../resources/planning-a-website.md#creating-a-visual-sitemap-with-drawio) section of the resource. Connect shapes by dragging from the blue arrows that appear when you hover over a shape.

## Plan the Navigation

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using your sitemap**, write down the navigation plan for the Paws and Claws website:

    - the links that will appear in the navigation bar, in order
    - any links that will appear inside page content
    - any image links

??? question "Hint"
    The top-level pages in the sitemap become the navigation links. Look back at your requirements list: the client asked for one link inside the home page content, and one image that works as a link.

## A Second Brief

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using a document and draw.io**, repeat the three exercises above for a new client: write the requirements list, draw the sitemap, and plan the navigation.

    !!! quote "Client brief: Riverside Bike Repairs"
        We're a bike repair workshop and we need a simple website. We want a home page, a page listing our repair services, a page selling bike accessories, and a page with our contact details.

        On the home page we want a short introduction, our address and phone number, and our opening hours shown in a table. There should also be a link from the home page to the repairs page.

        The repairs page should have a table of repairs with the price of each one. The accessories page should show each product with a photo, name and price.

        The contact page should have our address and a form with fields for a name, a phone number, an email address, the type of bike and a message.

        Please use our logo on every page and have it link back to the home page, and make sure all the pages have the same menu.

??? question "Hint"
    This brief uses different wording for the same kinds of requirements. Notice the requirements that are different from the Paws and Claws brief, such as a table on the repairs page and extra form fields on the contact page.

## Choose a Sitemap Type

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using the Planning a Website resource**, decide which type of sitemap suits each situation below, and name a tool you could use to create it.

    - A designer needs to show a client how the pages of a new website will be organised, before any code is written.
    - A business wants to help Google find every page on its 200-page website.
    - A large online shop wants a page visitors can use to find any section of the site.

??? question "Hint"
    Compare each situation with the **Who it is for** column of the Types of Sitemaps table: clients and developers, search engines, or visitors.

## Sitemap Considerations

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using your Riverside Bike Repairs sitemap**, answer each question below in a sentence:

    - Does the sitemap include every page the client asked for?
    - Who is the target audience, and what will they most want to find?
    - How many clicks does it take to reach each page from the home page?
    - Do all the file names follow the naming rules?
    - If the client wanted to add a blog later, where would it fit?

??? question "Hint"
    Compare your sitemap against your requirements list line by line. The [What to Consider](../resources/planning-a-website.md#what-to-consider-when-creating-a-sitemap) table in the resource explains each consideration.

## Wireframe a Page

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Using pen and paper or draw.io**, draw a wireframe of the Paws and Claws **home page**, showing where the logo, navigation, headings, welcome text, address, opening hours table, services link and footer will go.

??? question "Hint"
    A wireframe uses plain boxes and labels, with no colours or real images. Draw a box with a cross through it to show where an image goes, and wavy lines to show text.

## Confirm the Plan

!!! abstract "Instructions"
    **Working with a classmate**, swap your Paws and Claws requirements list and sitemap.

    Pretend your classmate is the client. They must check your plan against the brief and tell you anything missing or incorrect. Record their feedback, update your plan, and ask them to sign and date it to show they approve.

??? question "Hint"
    This is how plans are confirmed at work: the client or supervisor reviews the plan before any building starts. Ask your classmate to read the original brief again as they check, rather than working from memory.
